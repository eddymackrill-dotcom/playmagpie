## Part 2: the production system

### 2.1 Template spec, and the body-swap problem solved by construction

Every regulatory page (any format) carries these REQUIRED elements,
enforced by a typed data layer (lib/regulatory-content.ts, Record over
an as-const slug union, no fallback, the Batch 1/2 mechanics):

- Status line: the pipeline of dated stages with the current stage
  marked ("Gazetted 27 Mar 2026 -> committee -> vote -> assent").
- "As of [date]" statement in the lead AND on every claim block that
  can move.
- Player-impact verdict callout, maintained, re-dated at every update,
  INCLUDING the reverse case: where players DO carry exposure (Vietnam,
  Japan), the verdict says so plainly. The operator-vs-player line is
  stated in both directions, never assumed player-safe.
- Sources list, one entry per load-bearing claim (verify-or-omit: a
  claim without a source is cut, and "not documented" or "unverified"
  is written where the gap is itself informative).
- Update promise line + reviewBy date (registered in the data layer,
  surfaced to the maintenance rota).

**The body-swap problem, solved structurally rather than by effort.**
The June 2026 demotion hit name-swap templating: identical structure,
one noun rotated. The defence at volume is that STRUCTURE IS DERIVED
FROM THE JURISDICTION'S FACTS, not from a master layout:

1. **Mechanic-led section ordering.** Each jurisdiction is classified
   by its dominant regulatory mechanic: criminalisation-led (Japan,
   Vietnam), licensing-transition-led (Ireland, Sri Lanka, Finland),
   payment/blocking-led (Brazil, India, Germany, NL), tax-led (Uganda,
   Kenya, Colombia), litigation-led (US prediction markets, South
   Africa), event-led (Thailand, UAE). The mechanic determines which
   section leads the page and what the H1 asserts. Two pages share a
   skeleton only if their jurisdictions genuinely share a mechanic,
   and even then their leads carry different statutes and dates.
2. **The falsifiability gate (the Batch 1 rule, made mandatory):**
   title, H1 and lead must each contain at least one
   jurisdiction-specific fact (statute number, date, figure, body
   name) such that swapping the jurisdiction name makes the sentence
   FALSE, not merely generic. This is a per-page checklist item at the
   commit gate, not an aspiration.
3. **Automated similarity gate.** Before any regulatory page commits,
   a script (scripts/similarity-gate.mjs, to be written at programme
   start) shingles the new page's prose (5-gram) against every
   existing regulatory page and fails the commit above 30% overlap
   with any single page: the existing volume-safeguard rule
   ("no two pages in the same week may share more than 30% structural
   similarity") turned from judgement into tooling. Boilerplate that
   MUST repeat (the update-promise line, disclaimer) is whitelisted
   by exact string and excluded from the calculation.
4. **No generated prose.** The data layer holds hand-written strings
   per jurisdiction; there is no `Best {X} 2026` interpolation
   anywhere in the family. The template supplies layout and
   discipline; every sentence is written for its jurisdiction.

### 2.2 Build workflow

Radar event -> queue entry -> build session:

1. RESEARCH (autonomous): primary source located (statute, gazette,
   court order, regulator release); two independent secondaries where
   the primary is unfetchable; anything unverifiable marked for
   omission. Time-box: 45 min per single page; a page that cannot be
   sourced inside the box goes back to the queue tagged
   "source-blocked" rather than shipping thin.
2. DRAFT (autonomous): template spec above; length by format
   (single page 500-900 words; tracker seed 300-500 + log; matrix
   rows 1-2 sentences each).
3. GATE (autonomous, mechanical): falsifiability check on title/H1/
   lead; similarity script; em-dash and banned-phrase greps; title
   <=60 / meta <=160 / title != h1; sources list complete;
   reviewBy set.
4. COMMIT + PUSH + LASTMOD (autonomous): per-page `modified` in the
   data layer; separate commit per page (existing rule); push is
   deploy.
5. BING DISPATCH (owner-side, the only manual step): new URLs submit
   automatically via the sitemap diff; UPDATES need workflow_dispatch,
   batched to the 10-URL cap. The session's report ends with the exact
   dispatch line(s), as now.

**What needs owner approval: nothing per-page** (autonomy rule,
2026-08-01). The owner-level decisions are: the Part 3 cap/domain
fork; monthly slate size while any cap stands; reopening anything the
emergency bar or standing protections cover. Everything else ships and
is reported.

### 2.3 Format mix and the picker

| Format | Shape | Cost | When the workflow picks it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-jurisdiction page | /country/X/legal or /regulatory/X | 1 URL, 45-90 min, one reviewBy | A jurisdiction with a stable-but-moving position and enough verified substance for 500+ words (Ireland, Sri Lanka, Spain, Uganda) |
| Timeline tracker | /tracker/story | 1 URL, seed + ~30 min per update | One story, many dated events, fast cadence (prediction markets, Thailand bill, AU-style national bills) |
| Jurisdiction-matrix tracker | /tracker/x-by-jurisdiction | 1 URL, ~10-15 min per row seeded, per-row upkeep | Many parallel jurisdictions asked the same question (US iGaming/sweeps states, Canadian provinces, African tax wave, LatAm rollout) |

Matrix trackers are the breadth engine: a 30-row matrix is one URL and
one sitemap entry. Their template is in the strategy addendum (A4):
per-row status/date/impact/source, per-row as-of dates, staleness
downgrade visible on-page. Picker rule of thumb: if the query is
"in my state/province/country X" over a shared question, matrix; if
the query is "what is happening with [story]", timeline; if the
query is "is it legal in X and what else should I know", single page.

### 2.4 Maintenance at volume, with the honest ceiling

- Every artefact carries reviewBy in the data layer. A weekly rota
  step (RUNBOOK, Part 5) lists artefacts due; the monthly audit
  re-verifies everything 90+ days old.
- Staleness rule (binding): single pages and timeline trackers whose
  newest dated element exceeds 60 days at a weekly check are updated
  that week or archived with a dated closing entry. Matrix rows over
  90 days are re-verified or visibly downgraded to "unverified since
  [date]"; a matrix over 20% downgraded is archived.
- Automated currency check (to build alongside the similarity gate):
  a script that lists every `modified`/reviewBy in the regulatory
  data layer against today's date and emits the due list; run at
  session start by rota habit. No scraping, no auto-editing: it
  schedules human verification, nothing else.

**The ceiling, stated with numbers.** Re-verifying a single page or
tracker takes 20-40 minutes done honestly (re-pull the primary, check
the pipeline moved, re-date the verdict). A matrix row takes ~10.
At a realistic maintenance budget of ~4 focused hours/week on top of
new builds, the rota sustains roughly 8-10 page-verifications weekly,
which on a 60-90 day cycle supports **on the order of 60-90 discrete
URLs plus 3-5 matrices (100-150 rows) in steady state**. Beyond that,
either the maintenance budget rises, the archive rate rises, or
quality drops: and the one thing this programme cannot do is leave
stale claims live, because unsourced-or-stale is exactly what kills
citation value. Total sustainable estate under this plan: ~100 URLs
of regulatory content, reached over 2-3 quarters, not 2-3 weeks.

## Part 3: Google risk, once, then options

**The risk, once:** the .com is under an algorithmic scaled-content
demotion, and this programme proposes publishing dozens of
structurally-related pages: the exact silhouette the June 2026
classifier flagged, on the domain it flagged. The 4/month cap exists
because publishing velocity on this domain was diagnosed as the
trigger variable. High-volume regulatory publishing on the .com is a
real bet against that diagnosis, mitigated but not eliminated by the
anti-templating engineering in Part 2. Stated; now the options.

**Option A: raise or remove the .com cap.**
Fastest, uses the domain where the earning channel (citations ->
assistant sessions -> FTDs) already lives, compounds the existing
flywheel (the site already earns citations on legality content).
Trade-off: maximum Google-recovery risk; if the next spam update
re-runs the classifier against 60 new same-family URLs, the
suppression could harden. Mitigable by pacing (e.g. 12/month, not
40), the Part 2 gates, and URL-frugal formats: but the risk is real
and this option accepts it.

**Option B: build on playmagpie.co.uk.**
Clean domain, no demotion, but the wrong vehicle: it is a
UKGC-facing compliance property whose entire posture is
licensed-UK-content-only. Loading it with global offshore-gambling
legality content would undercut the compliance identity that is its
reason to exist, and its rules (unchanged by the .com autonomy
decisions, per CLAUDE.md) would need owner-side rewriting first.
Viable only for UK-scope regulatory content (white paper, levy, FRA),
which it should arguably carry anyway. Not a home for the global
programme.

**Option C: new dedicated domain (a regulatory-tracker brand).**
Zero demotion baggage, no cap, clean separation of the volume bet
from the .com's recovery. Trade-offs: starts with zero authority and
zero citations flywheel (though the AI/Bing channel rewarded .com
pages within DAYS, which suggests the channel is authority-light and
freshness/specificity-driven: the Australia surge is evidence a new
domain could catch quickly); operational cost of a second property
(hosting, WMT, dispatch workflow, STATE discipline); splits the
maintenance rota across two estates; and any citations it earns do
not accrue to the domain that carries the affiliate funnel.

**Option D: matrix-heavy on the .com inside the current cap.**
Breadth through rows, not URLs: two or three matrix trackers plus a
handful of timeline trackers cover 40+ jurisdictions in under 8 URLs,
fully inside existing velocity rules. Trade-off: single-jurisdiction
depth (the Australia-page shape that actually caught) is rationed;
matrices may citation-perform differently than deep pages, and that
is precisely untested.

**Recommendation: D now, A on data, C in reserve.** Ship the
September pilot plus 2 matrix trackers inside the existing cap
(Option D costs nothing it isn't already spending); read the 30-day
citation data; if hits cluster (especially if matrices perform), take
the cap decision (Option A at a stated pace, e.g. 12/month
regulatory) with evidence instead of forecast. If at that point the
Google-recovery fear outweighs the channel evidence, Option C is the
pressure valve: move VOLUME to a new domain and keep the .com's
regulatory estate at boutique size. Noted as the owner requested:
the Bing/AI channel, which is the one producing customers, has shown
no sign of penalising this content: every signal to date (Australia,
the pending guide, the citation spike) rewards it. This is the
owner's call; the data gate makes it a better-informed one in ~6
weeks.

   the 60-day page rule and 90-day matrix-row rule (update, or

   record citations per regulatory artefact in

   and what correlates (format? jurisdiction size? event type?

   product: it converts the breadth bet into knowledge.
