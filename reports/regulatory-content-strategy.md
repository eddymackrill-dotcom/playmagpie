## Part 3: the Regulatory Tracker format

**Verdict: the tracker model fits this site; a news feed does not. But
the honest version of this evaluation is that we have already built one
tracker without naming it: the Australia legal page's dated update
section IS the format, proven at 347 citations. The design below
generalises it; the open question is placement, not mechanics.**

**Placement recommendation: /tracker/[slug] as a new allowlisted static
family** (same single-source as-const allowlist + Record discipline as
compare/bonus; dynamicParams false). Not under /guides (a tracker is
not evergreen prose and the dated-log format would look broken there)
and not under /country (US-sweeps and Curaçao stories are not
countries). One URL per story, created once, zero URL cost thereafter.

**Template spec:**
- Title: story-led, no year-stamp ("US Sweepstakes Casino Crackdown:
  State-by-State Tracker"); H1 differs per house rule.
- Status line block at top: the pipeline rendered as dated stages
  ("Introduced 2 Jul -> Senate committee report due 17 Aug -> Senate
  vote -> commencement 1 Jan 2027"), with the current stage marked.
  This is the AI-extractable artefact and the thing no static
  trade-press article carries.
- Maintained player-impact verdict directly under the status line, in
  a callout, re-dated at every update: the "what this changes for a
  player" discipline from the Australia section. This is the
  differentiator versus trade press, which writes for operators.
- Dated update log, newest first, each entry 60-150 words with its own
  "as of" date and source link. Entries are append-only; corrections
  get a dated correction entry, never a silent rewrite (site
  corrections policy applies).
- Sources list at the foot, growing with the log; every entry's claim
  traces to it (verify-or-omit).
- Schema: Article with dateModified = last entry date; NO FAQPage
  (rich results are revoked sitewide and FAQ content belongs to the
  country/legal pages; keeps the cannibalisation boundary clean).
  Breadcrumbs as site-standard.
- Data layer: lib/tracker-content.ts, per-slug `modified` feeding the
  sitemap, reviewBy REQUIRED on every tracker (see kill rule).

**Update workflow (per event):** radar hit (weekly check step or news
encounter) -> draft entry -> verify to primary source or named
reputable secondary; anything unverifiable is omitted -> append entry +
re-date the impact verdict + bump `modified` in the same commit ->
push -> single-URL workflow_dispatch to Bing. Cost per update: well
under an hour. The whole loop is this session's Australia mechanic.

**Tracker vs country legal page, the cannibalisation rule:** where both
exist, the LEGAL PAGE is canonical for the legal position and its
query cluster ("is it legal in X", tax, statutes); the TRACKER is
canonical for the live story cluster ("what is happening with X bill",
"which states banned X", "did X pass"). The legal page's update
section shrinks to a two-line pointer plus link once a tracker exists
for its market; the tracker links back to the legal page for the
stable position. One direction of authority each way, no restated
content. Where no legal page exists (US, Curaçao), the tracker stands
alone and carries a short static "the legal position today" block.

**Homepage "Latest regulatory updates" block:** technically clean:
the homepage is not a protected page, a 3-4 line dated-links block is
one bounded component, and it can ship without touching any protected
page. Google-risk: low as specified (it is internal linking with
fresh anchor text, not new URLs; homepage lastmod already floats).
Value: it hands the site's freshest signals to the most-crawled URL
and gives trackers their carrier links for free. Recommendation: build
it WITH the first tracker (its carrier link), not before; cap it at 4
line-items, hand-maintained, no auto-feed.

**Honest assessment of the model:** single-URL freshness accumulation
is the right shape for a spam-demoted domain (each update deepens one
URL rather than minting thin new ones; the Australia page proves
accumulated authority on one URL compounds). Cap cost is one slot per
tracker at creation, zero after. The failure mode is real: an
abandoned tracker with a stale "as of" line is worse than no page,
because it advertises unreliability on exactly the trust surface we
sell. **Kill rule, binding: every tracker carries reviewBy; a tracker
whose latest entry is older than 60 days at any weekly check is
updated that week or archived (a dated final entry stating the story
has gone quiet and the page is closed, kept live but marked resolved).
Never silently stale.**

it. The legal page remains the canonical and only home.** It holds 347
citations, the prime directive protects it, its update section already
performs the tracker function, and a second URL on the same story would
split the exact query cluster we currently win (the cannibalisation
rule above, applied to ourselves). The 17 Aug Senate report lands as
the next dated entry in the existing update section under the standing
2026-09-01 tripwire. One home, not two.

**Pilot composition (3 new URLs + 1 zero-cost update), September:**

1. **Zero-cost, immediate, not really "pilot": the Canada/Alberta
   additive update.** Overdue since 13 July. Australia playbook: dated
   section on /country/canada/legal ("Update, August 2026: Alberta's
   market is live"), launch date, 22 operators, AGLC/AiGC structure,
   what changes for a player outside the licensed market, lastmod bump,
   single-URL dispatch. No cap cost. Should ship this week regardless
   of the pilot decision.
2. **Pilot page A (vertical): /guides/source-of-funds-checks-at-crypto-
   casinos.** Brief: what triggers a source-of-funds request, the
   document classes operators actually accept, our owner-verified
   thresholds where they exist and "not documented" where they do not,
   what happens to the balance during review, the honest "no verified
   turnaround exists for any operator we cover" line. Scope-split
   statements against the KYC guide and large-withdrawals guide.
   Carrier: prose link from /guides/large-crypto-casino-withdrawals
   (same-commit modification). reviewBy: quarterly.
3. **Pilot page B (Tier 1): /country/ireland/legal.** Brief: the
   GRAI regime as of now (remote betting licences issuing since 1 July

4. **Pilot tracker: /tracker/us-sweepstakes-casino-crackdown.** Brief:
   status line (multi-state pipeline: banned states count, pending

   all 8 catalogue operators restrict the US and the page says so),

**Cap fork, an owner decision (the brief cut off here; both options
stated with a recommendation):** September's 4-slot cap must cover
pilot pages A + B + the tracker (3 slots) while NZ-legal is slated for
November. Option 1: run the full pilot in September (3 of 4 slots,
one spare), NZ in November as planned; session cap 2 means the pilot
ships across two sessions. Option 2: halve the pilot (tracker + one
static) to keep two September slots free. **Recommendation: Option 1.
The month has no other queued builds, the spare slot covers a
surprise, and a 3-page pilot is the minimum that tests all three
content classes (vertical, catalogue-legal, tracker) in one
measurement window.**

**Measurement, 30 days per page via Bing AI Performance (owner pull):**
citation count and share on each page's query cluster, plus appearance
in Search Performance keywords. Baselines: zero for all three (new
URLs).

**Scale criteria (any of):** any pilot page reaches 15+ citations in
30 days on its cluster (the pending-guide benchmark: 16 with no event);
OR the tracker's citation count responds within a week to a dated
update twice in the window (the Australia signature). If met: scale to
a 10+/quarter programme (2-3 trackers, remaining Tier 1 calendar,
verticals), each addition still individually gated on the standard
rules.

**Kill criteria (all measured at 30 days):** all three pages under 5
citations AND no Search Performance keyword rows AND the tracker's
updates produce no citation movement. If met: stop the expansion
programme, keep the Tier 1 catalogue calendar (Australia/Alberta/NZ/
Ireland/Finland are justified by the existing channel regardless), and
archive the tracker under the kill rule. A mixed result (one page
performs) scales only the class that performed.

**What this pilot deliberately does not test:** UK content (owner
boundary decision), Brazil/India builds (non-English or authority-risk
constraints), and any Google-side effect (the suppression makes Google
unmeasurable; this is a Bing/AI-channel programme and its metrics are
citations, not positions, consistent with the one-off exception framing
recorded in STATE.md on 2026-08-11).
